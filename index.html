Cycle Structure›

The program runs on an 8-day rotating cycle — Push / Pull / Legs / Rest / Push / Pull / Legs / Rest — deliberately de-coupled from the calendar week. Training frequency is driven by recovery, not the calendar.

Three consecutive PPL days is tolerable because each session targets largely non-overlapping muscle groups. Each muscle is trained approximately every 4 days — closer to twice-weekly frequency than once.

If you miss a session, pick up exactly where you left off. Never cram two sessions into one day to catch up, and never skip a session to stay calendar-aligned.
Intensity — Compounds vs Isolations›

Compounds: graduated RIR across sets — S1 at 2–3 RIR, S2 at 1–2 RIR, S3 at 0–1 RIR. Stop one rep before technical breakdown. Form breakdown on a heavy squat or RDL is where injuries happen.

Isolations: true failure (0 RIR) across all sets. Failure on a leg extension, cable curl, or lateral raise carries no meaningful injury risk and maximises the stimulus-to-fatigue ratio.

Progressive Overload›

Double progression is the primary overload method:

  • Hit the top of the rep range across all sets at the prescribed RIR → add weight next session (2.5kg upper / 5kg lower)
  • Adding weight drops you back to the lower end of the range — work back up over subsequent sessions
  • For high-rep isolations (15–20), use smaller increments where plates aren't practical
  • For unilateral movements, let the weaker side determine the load
Calf Training›

Both leg sessions use standing raises exclusively. 2023 research found standing raises produced significantly greater gastrocnemius hypertrophy than seated, while soleus growth was comparable between both.

Vary toe angle across sets: neutral covers both heads evenly, toes slightly out biases the medial head, toes slightly in biases the lateral head.
Elbow Management›

Overhead tricep extension has been removed entirely — the overhead stretched position under load is the primary aggravating mechanism for the right distal triceps tendon. Rope pushdowns are the sole direct tricep isolation, loading the tricep at a comfortable angle.

Monitor elbow response to accumulated push volume. If irritation increases, reduce pushdown sets first. Consider a sports physio for a specific eccentric loading protocol — tendons require progressive load to remodel.

Warm-Up & Cardio›

General warm-up: 5 minutes treadmill at 2–4% incline, 5.5–6.5 km/h. Noticeably warmer and breathing slightly elevated before touching a barbell.

Compound pyramid: every primary compound needs its own warm-up set sequence before working sets:

  • Set 1 — 40–50% working weight, 8–10 reps. Pattern only, zero fatigue.
  • Set 2 — 60–70% working weight, 4–5 reps.
  • Set 3 — 80–90% working weight, 2–3 reps.
  • Rest 2 minutes after the final warm-up set, then begin working sets.

Secondary compounds later in the session — 1 lighter set only. Isolations — nothing needed.

Pull-up warm-up (specific): bodyweight is ~95% of working weight at current +5kg loading, so a full pyramid would arrive at near-working intensity. Instead: 1 set scapular pulls (8–10 reps) + 3–4 bodyweight pull-ups at easy effort. Straight into weighted sets.

Cardio: after Push or Pull sessions only — never after Legs. 15–20 min, 2–3× per week.

Incline: 10–12% · Speed: 5.5–6.5 km/h · Duration: 15–20 min. If resistance session quality declines, trim cardio to 12–15 min before touching the resistance program.
Deload Protocol›

After every 4–5 cycles (~5–6 weeks), run a deload. Keep all exercises — reduce every session to 1–2 sets per exercise and drop load by 30–40%.

Not optional at advanced training intensity. Accumulated joint and central fatigue blunts progress and increases injury risk. The distal triceps tendon specifically benefits from unloading regardless of current symptom status.
